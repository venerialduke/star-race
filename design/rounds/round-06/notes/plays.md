# Notes: The player

## Did the notes land

Yes, every one. The opening is three summaries, not one tension. Speed is now Thrust plus a holding speed plus a corner plan, and all three of the owner's threads are in: Lift/Carry/Charge, overshoot into the other branch, Nav goals. Taps are gone and abilities fire themselves, with Rake untargeted and Mines pre-placed exactly as the note suggested. Crew maintenance, splits, the set points line, points versus credits, Hold the Line, interest, pit stops, between-lap shops, rotating rivals: all present and each named in "What changed." Wanted and cops are removed and listed, not lost. The two reader items from last round are handled the way the brief asked. The open calls are about systems, except "how does a group fill when survivors do not divide by three," which is in the weeds again.

## Does the idea survive

It is the same design with the notes worked in. Same ship, same path, same pools, same swing, same crews. Two things changed shape, and the document says so: the race asks for nothing from the thumb now, and a heat is three separate lap-races instead of one three-lap duel. The seed's core, fast means less reaction and swinging wide is the danger, is intact. Every seed system is accounted for. Two small things slipped: the seed's nav "gravity assists" and "improves handling" are gone without a word, and the seed wanted "some randomness" in the cut, which is flagged as refused rather than altered. That is honest, but say it once in the seed's own terms.

## What works

The corner plan. "Lift brakes to the holding speed before the bend: no swing, some time lost" is a choice I can make in a five-minute gap and watch pay off or not. Paired with crew type, it produces the whole bill: Charge on a humanoid crew is a story and an invoice.

Overshoot into the other branch. "A wild Thrust build on Charge can do this on purpose" is the sentence I would build a ship around.

Points and credits are finally two words for two things. Hold the Line against the Long Line is readable in one glance.

## What does not

The screen. With taps gone, a lap is a replay. That is fine for build-and-watch, but auto-chess fights last thirty seconds; this is three laps, three pit stops, three shops, and the document never says how long a heat takes. The base said a couple of minutes. Say it again.

Two invisible stats now decide races. Reaction time governs "when to fire, how far to shift shields, how full a pocket fills, which way to go at a dark fork," and none of it shows. Add the swing and I lose to two numbers I cannot see and one die I cannot see.

"Every ship on the course at once, three ships to a group" is ambiguous. Nine dots on one loop with my three highlighted, or three loops? The fixture rule implies three loops. On a phone this decides whether I can follow my race at all.

The pit stop "at the cost of a beat" makes the only in-race act a penalty. Skipping it is the default play, so the one thumb has nothing to do.

Jargon I guessed at: "beat" (a unit of time lost, never defined), "pocket" (never defined here), "the well opens" (in the worked heat, undefined), "sealed junction," "pace ship." And Scoop is both an ability and half of "Dark Matter Scoop," a collector. Two things, one word.

## What I would add

A one-line verdict after every lap: "swung wide at bend four: Charge, bad draw." That is the whole legibility fix and it costs nothing.

A reaction gauge on the ship that visibly falls as it speeds up, so the seed's trade is seen, not only simulated.

Make the pit stop free to open and charge the beat only if you change something.

Fold splits into fixtures as a fifth fixture type, "Fork," so the shop has one tab for shaping the course.

## What I would cut first

Splits. Two kinds, two durations, and an unresolved question about whether they reshape a rival's heat. What you lose is the permanent long hold and the note's "add a track section." A Fork fixture keeps most of that. Second on the list is the nav goal, which overlaps the corner plan and the route; what you would lose is Nav being "what the ship is for," which the corner plan already half says.
