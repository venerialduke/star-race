# Notes: The player

## Did the notes land

Yes, all seven, and visibly. Jargon: "lands" is gone with its mechanic, Rake is defined in the sentence that introduces it, and elimination is one plain sentence in the season section. Numbers: every tick, point and credit sum is out; what is left is counts and one ratio. Handling: reaction lag is removed and the swing replaces it, and the document says outright that the base's premise moved. Reaction time: derived from speed, acceleration and crew, governing shields, pockets and unplanned forks, with the timing-window extension taken up. Tapping: zero to three, with a stated why. Route planning: clear, dim and dark forks, Nav lighting them. Laps: three, growing each lap, and the note's own sentence about variation is nearly quoted. Nothing was argued with; everything was applied.

## Does the idea survive

It is the same design. Golden path, six parts, two crews, fixtures carried into every heat, purse and pools, wanted and cutters, margin bonus, Long Line, nine players and two cuts: all still here in the same words. The one thing that moved was told to move, and the document names it. Wide Line became a fork on the board, which is the note's route planning doing the same job; Scoop was added so a wide build has a tap. That is notes worked in, not a new game.

Against the seed, every named system is accounted for except one phrase: the nav system "optimizing acceleration, using gravity assists" is gone without a word. Nav now lights forks, picks the side of a swing, and flies a dead ship home; it no longer plans the fast line. Small, but it vanished rather than being flagged. Passive collectors, randomness at the cut and "mods only for your own races" are all flagged, as they should be.

## What works

"The swing is not a delay, it is weather you dress for." That is the sentence the round was for. Throttle is one dial that touches swing, reaction, tap window and gravity at once, so the plan on the board is real: how much speed can I carry into bends I cannot see. That is a plan worth making in a five-minute gap.

"Not tapping is always fine." This is the right rule for build-and-watch on a phone. I can watch a heat on the train with my thumb in my pocket and the ship is still mine. The tap is an upgrade, not a duty.

Nav picking the side a wide swing lands on. It turns a bad draw into a story I can tell: I swung wide into the pocket, not the mine, because I paid for Nav.

The worked heat retells itself in one breath: bad draw on lap one, blind pick paid on lap two, missed the second Rake window on lap three, close third. That is a heat with a story and a lesson.

## What does not

Can I see why I lost? The swing is a seeded draw and the spread is invisible. When I go wide on lap one I do not know if it was my throttle, my Handling or the dice. The document leaves the spread band as an open call. It is not open. Felt-only luck on a two-minute race reads as the game cheating.

The board is eight decisions: parts, throttle, crew, two of four actives, two of three collectors, two of four fixtures placed, a route at every fork, Long Line yes or no. Five slots of "fit two of N" before a two-minute heat. On a phone the board becomes the race and the race becomes the reward screen.

Two buttons with windows. Brace fires before a bend, Rake when a rival is near, Burn on a straight, Scoop in a pocket. With two fitted, the screen must tell me which window is coming and I must hit the right button in a window that narrows with speed. That is a rhythm game grafted on with one thumb.

Words I had to guess at. "Collector" means the pool equipment in one paragraph and the thing a pocket fills in another; "Scoop" is both an active and a collector, so "tap Scoop to fill the Scoop" is a real sentence in this design. "Loses a length", "costs the ship a beat", "the season seed" are never defined. "A blind fork" is used in the Crew section before forks exist.

Gravity has no trigger. Throttle is held, so when does a ship "burn hard out of every bend"? The obvious answer, re-accelerating after every swing at high throttle, is never said, and it is the link that would make gravity a consequence of the swing rather than a separate rule.

The rivals matter on the board, through fixtures and pools, and barely on the track. Until a Rake, the other two ships are scenery.

## What I would add

Show the spread band at every bend for the throttle set, and after the heat a one-line ledger per lost length: draw, throttle, fixture or fork.

One button. The approaching window names which fitted active it belongs to; the thumb only says "now".

State the gravity trigger: every wide swing costs a hard burn to rejoin, and that burn is the gravity. Then humanoid versus robot bites exactly where the core tension lives.

Fold the two growth axes into one: the sections that opened this stage stay open next stage. The base loop grows by what the players already raced.

Rename the pool equipment and the active so no word does two jobs.

Answer two open calls: dark forks stay the crew's call, because that is what makes Nav worth buying; Rake needs the same line, because it makes wide runners hard to hit and gives the third lap a reason to be close.

## What I would cut first

The three pools and their three collectors. The document already argues the winner is richest and the pools are capped beneath the purse, so they are a second economy that pays less than the thing I am already trying to do, at the price of a fit slot, three nouns and a cap rule. Replace them with flat payments: a pocket pays whoever passes through it and empties as it is drawn, so it is still shared; a Rake hit pays a bounty on its own. What you lose is the seed's guess about what the other two fitted, and that is a real loss, because it is one of the few ways the rivals reach the board. Second on the list: the cutter. Keep wanted status as a price on your own head, paying rivals more for hitting you, and drop the police ship AI entirely.
