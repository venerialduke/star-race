# Critique

1. **It never says whether intensity persists across heats, and the saturation answer fails either way.** Each garage stakes the next course and course A is "seeded at intensity 1", which reads as a fresh course each heat; but the defence — "stops the course saturating", "ratcheting to four everywhere by heat 4", "the flare goes quiet for the rest of the season" — only makes sense if intensity carries. If it resets, one Board of eight moves against a headroom of twelve (four sockets, 1 to 4) fills most of it in a single garage, and from heat 4 an ignited raise counts two. If it carries, it is saturated by heat 3. The base proposal's fatal flaw was saturation; the fix here is one credit and a free clear, and the arithmetic is never done.

2. **Three of the four ignitions do not survive contact with `tuning.ts`.** Ward: duration 90 + 25 per blue is 190 ticks at four blue, against a cooldown of 140 — and `actives.ts` starts a cooldown at the moment of firing (`readyAgainAt = tick + cooldownTicks`), so shields never drop and the pool refills every 140 ticks. That is permanent immunity, not "56% of a course"; the 56% came from interaction-first's 180 duration and 320 cycle, which assumed a cooldown measured from the drop. Torch: at cooldown 210 and duration 120 a ship fires at ticks 0, 210, 420, 630 and 840 — five boosts on a 950-tick heat, not two, and the 90 idle ticks shed 180 heat against the 96 a boost costs, so it is free. Keel: `ASTEROID_DAMAGE_PER_TICK` is 0.12, so a cap of 2 hull per tick binds only above about speed 2.0 on a build whose cells cost speed. The armour build's online moment is a no-op.

3. **The credit table cannot be produced by the stated income rules.** Garage 4 shows a bank of 12 at the deal off a bank of 6, interest +2 and ante −3, which needs a purse of 11 when the maximum purse is 8. Garages 2, 6 and 7 each compute interest off a number that matches neither the bank before the purse nor after it, and the implied finishes are 6, 5, 11, 6, 7, 6 — second and third place most heats, not "a mid-field ship". Everything in "why spending is not obviously right" rests on this table, so the claim that neither the spender nor the saver is right is currently unsupported.

4. **Nine modifiers, not eight, and "each Drive owns exactly two numbers" is false.** Sling owns three: pull per purple cell, the ×1.25 inside a hole, and the ignored escape threshold — the last is a separate branch in `blackHole()`, not a value the pull number can carry. The engineering review marked Ignition down for exactly nine with an unproven ceiling; the synthesis inherits the ninth and calls the cap structural.

5. **Intensity as a placement count breaks a rule from "What survives from the slice".** `DESIGN.md` requires a placement to lie entirely inside its segment. A course is 800 to 950 ticks over five or six segments, so a segment averages about 160 ticks, and "the number of bursts 150 ticks apart" at intensity 4 needs 450-plus ticks in one socket. Either the spacing collapses, or segments triple in length, or placements spill across segment boundaries — and the synthesis flags this as its own PR without noticing the geometry does not fit.

6. **Grey cells are a cheaper repair than the patch, which undoes hull as run health.** A part that raises maximum hull adds it to the ship as it stands, so Ablative Plating is 40 hull for 4 credits plus a permanent +40 max plus a Keel pip; the patch is 40 hull for 3 credits _and_ your one purchase. The patch is dominated by a tier-1 cell for any ship not at its slot cap, which is every ship before garage 6 — including the player, who patches at garage 4 in the worked run. "Hull carries, never regenerates, and the only cure is a patch that costs a cell" is not true of the cell list the same document prints.

7. **The counter ring is a ring of offence; being targeted still has no build answer that is not the attacker's own colour.** Every arrow says "my hazard hurts colour X worst", so reading four grey pips tells you to buy purple in order to hurt the Keels — a weapon, not armour. A Torch under rock at 4 can clear once for free, retaliate with the flare, or buy grey and un-do its own speed. The brief asks how another player's build changes _my build_; the answer here changes who I attack, and round-01's complaint that no proposal names a Y that is not X survives one level down.

8. **The worked run's final points are impossible.** Nine minus position gives the player 4, 7, 8, 7, 8 across heats 1 to 5, which is 34; Bulwark wins heat 6, capping the player at 7 more. The ceiling is 41 and the standings say 44, so either a position in the narrative or the points rule is wrong — and the head-start ladder, 40/25/12/0, is read straight off those totals.

9. **The Draft is still not specified, which is what round-01's critique asked for in full.** Eight cells face up, eight ships, one purchase each: it never says whether the cards are shared (so the leader, picking last, may find an empty board) or dealt per ship, whether a bought copy leaves this garage's deal, or what returns when a ship is cut. Tier 2 unlocks at garage 3, so garages 1 and 2 deal eight cards drawn from four distinct cells — a four-way choice, not a draft. And the first draft runs before any standings exist, so "worst-placed picks first" has no order at garage 1.

10. **The cost section omits the lobby, the seven courses and `actives.ts`, and contradicts itself on `pilot.ts`.** It lists `pilot.ts`'s tap rules as surviving unchanged and then says `actives.ts` stops being a static table "which reaches `pilot.ts` and the HUD" — and `pilot.ts` reads `ACTIVES.shields.durationTicks` today to aim its shield, so per-build durations are a rewrite of the one tap rule in the repo. Neither that PR nor the lobby screen (season map, ante schedule, counter ring) is in the seventeen. Seven courses of five or six segments with socket orders and splines is authoring work the slice needed a milestone for; twelve cells get a PR and seven courses get none.

11. **Whether the rivals are bots decides whether the Board is interaction, and it is left as owner question 1.** The design's skill is reading the ladder to predict the course, but the bot stake rule is published in three lines — raise if two or more ships are above you and it is under 4, clear what your Drive fears, otherwise hold — so against bots that read is arithmetic a player does twice and never again. The synthesis is right that a stake is an input either way; what it does not say is what the Board is worth when the field is deterministic, which is the brief's fourth gap asked plainly.

12. **The forced scrap can fail to pay, and one number is quoted against the wrong constant.** Six slots at 1 credit apiece raise at most 6 against a final ante of 8, so a broke finalist sells its whole build and still owes — undefined, and the design insists it has exactly one death rule. Separately, the escape threshold is given as 25 in the hull section; `BLACK_HOLE_ESCAPE_HULL` is 15, and a destroyed ship returning at 30 against a hole whose intensity lengthens the pull is never checked against either figure.

## What the next round should be asked

Fix the spine before extending it. Say in one sentence whether a socket's intensity resets with each course or carries down the season, then show the Board's arithmetic for eight ships, six ships and five: raises minus clears against a headroom of twelve, with ignited raises counting two, at heats 2, 4 and 6. If it saturates — and on the numbers as written it does — the price of a raise, the cap, or the number of ships that may raise has to change, and the fix has to be shown rather than asserted and handed to the harness.

Ask for the four Drives written against `tuning.ts` with the file open. Ward's shield cooldown is measured from the tap in `actives.ts`, so state the duration and cooldown that actually produce the uptime you want and say what uptime that is. Torch's boost count follows from cooldown, duration and heat dissipation of 2 per tick — give the count. Keel's cap has to sit inside the range that 0.12 per tick times speed squared times intensity actually reaches, so say what intensity does to the belt numerically first. One line per Drive: what it is worth in ticks on a 900-tick course carrying its own socket at 4, at three cells and at four.

Ask for the economy as a ledger that closes. Every row derivable from a stated finishing position, a stated purse, interest computed at a stated moment, and the ante — for a spender, a saver and a switcher, over seven garages, with the arithmetic checkable line by line. While there, notice that points and the purse are the same figure per heat, and either merge them deliberately or say why one number is being printed twice.

Ask for hull to be run health or drop the claim. Price a hull cell and a patch against each other so that one does not dominate, and decide whether a cell's hull is current hull, maximum only, or both. Then say what a returning wreck at 30 hull faces on the next course, against the escape threshold the game actually uses.

Ask for a defensive move that is not a copy of the attacker. Clear is free and subtracts one from a socket the field pushed to four; that is the only answer a targeted ship has, and it is not a build decision. Either give a colour something it can buy that answers the hazard aimed at it, or admit the ring is an attack graph and say what makes reading it worth the tap.

Ask for the Draft in full — shared or dealt, what leaves the pool on a purchase, what returns on a scrap or a cut, and the order at garage 1 before any ladder exists — and for the cost section to include the lobby, the seven courses as level data, and the `actives.ts` and `pilot.ts` PR that per-build durations force. Then re-run the worked run and make its points add up.
