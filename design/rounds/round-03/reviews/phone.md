# Review: The phone player

I play with one thumb, in five-minute gaps, usually standing up. I score two
things hardest. Can I hold the systems in my head between sessions without
re-reading a manual, and when the race ends, does the screen tell me why I lost
in less time than the race took. Everything else is downstream of those.

A note on the count before the scores. The seed asks for three defensive
layers, a crew that dies separately, a gravity penalty, four shared pools and a
margin ladder. That is six systems before you have drawn a track. Every
proposal here keeps all six. Only two of them price the cost of keeping all six,
and none of them cuts one. That is the round's shared failure and it shows up in
the Legibility column.

## The Ledger and the Line (economy-first)

**Build 4.** Hull, nav, crew and mounts with prices attached, and three real
directions the worked run names: the deterrent Barge, the generalist Runner, the
lance Skiff. A 540-credit ship you cannot afford until stage 2 is a plan
arriving. Half a point off because the throttle dial is set per heat and the
ship's identity mostly lives in the mounts, so the ship becomes itself at the
shop rather than on the track.

**Tradeoffs 5.** Charge is income, shields, boost and ammunition at once. That
single coupling does more work than any other line in the round: a ship under
fire bleeds money holding its shields up even when it never loses a place, so
damage is theft rather than delay. Throttle eats grip on a 0/1/3/5/8 curve,
wanted stars tax the line you were harvesting, and a toll only pays if someone
else shows up. Every good thing here has a bill.

**Interaction 5.** Rigs are public, lines are private, and the flare is 25
credits that costs a rival 60. Sabine changing Vex's race with one tap on the
manifest, never going near Vex on the track, is the round's cleanest
demonstration that other players matter.

**Stakes 4.** Cuts at 12/8/6, a salvage grant, and matching by adjacent
standings so margins bite against people you are actually near. Marked down for
two things. Points are mostly ledger, so a pilot can collect their way to a
season while finishing third — the proposal names this and does not fix it. And
the margin divisor `g` is drawn per heat and **announced afterwards**. On a
phone, a target you learn after the fact is not a target. I cannot race to it,
so it is noise with a formula attached.

**Legibility 3.** The economy is the most readable in the round: one sentence,
one rule, four pools, and a post-heat screen that shows the split and teaches
shared pools without a tutorial. "I took 40 of the 120 because two others were
in it" is exactly the sentence I want to leave a race with. But the ship is
worse than the proposal admits. Crew has **stamina (100) and crew HP as two
separate numbers**, which is never flagged. With hull, shields and charge that
is five bars, not four, and the risks section counts four. Add wanted stars,
seams, tolls, flares and seven mounts and the manual is long. Then the seat rule
asks me to hold three different course layouts inside one 45-second race and
know which lap my own claim pays on. I cannot do that standing on a train.

**Fit 5.** Deterministic, integer ticks, four live taps or three pre-programmed.
The robot's pre-programmed race is a genuinely thumbless mode, which is the
right instinct for this player.

**Buildable 5.** The only proposal that names its first playable, and it is
honestly small: one course, three pools, one hull, the throttle dial, two
mounts, four heats, no tolls, no seams, no wanted. The thesis survives that cut
intact, which is the proof the thesis is load-bearing.

**Measurable 5.** Pool shares, a stated points formula and three archetypes a
harness can play against each other. Whether deterrence dominates is a number
you can get in an afternoon.

**Best idea.** Banked charge is your income and spent charge is your speed and
your shields. One bar, four jobs, and mid-race the player watches that bar and
decides whether this heat buys a place or a payday. Nothing else in the round
gets so much game out of so little screen.

**Fatal flaw.** None fatal, but the seat rotation is the closest thing to it.
Lap 1 on A's course, lap 2 on B's, lap 3 on C's is three tracks in one minute.
It makes tolls resolve cleanly and it makes the race unreadable. Cut it: race
one course per heat, the host's.

**What it makes better in the seed.** Shared pools. The seed says routes are
shared and pay less when contested, and leaves it there. This gives it one
resolution rule — your cut is the pool times your share of what the field
harvested — applies it identically to four named routes, and puts the resulting
split on one screen after the heat. That screen is the answer to the seed's
open question about learning how contested a route is, because it teaches you
this heat what to do next heat.

**What it drops, and was that right.** Drops the crewless ship as a chosen
build, folding it into the robot crew. Right — a chosen crewless ship is a
fourth thing to explain for a build most people will never pick. Drops gravity
as a general impairment and keeps only "boosts cost stamina". Right in
direction, thin in execution: it is the least interesting of the four gravity
answers and it costs a whole extra crew bar to express something one number
could carry.

## The Line and the Wide (track-first)

**Build 3.** Six systems rated 1 to 5 off a price table is the most generic
build screen in the round. It works, and the worked run shows three real
directions, but buying ratings is not a plan coming together — nothing on the
ship has a shape, only a level.

**Tradeoffs 5.** Offset is one number from 0 to 100 that simultaneously prices
speed, charge and credits, and you cannot be in two places on it. That is the
cleanest statement of the seed's central tension anywhere here. G-load over 60
adding 8 ticks to every tap makes acceleration a real bill. `Hold` — zero
charge, coast 15 ticks, shed 30 G — is a free button with a genuine cost, which
is hard to design and lovely to play.

**Interaction 5.** Declare blind, reveal, then place. One guess and one informed
move. Learning that two rivals declared Solar in time to put a mine in the Solar
band, rather than reading it in a results screen, is the best answer in the
round to the seed's ninth question. Bounty pools scaling with standing makes the
leader a target by rule instead of by fudge.

**Stakes 4.** Cuts of the bottom three across three phases, an underdog grant,
and a margin formula with a floor. Meridian reaching second on four heats of
8/7/8/7 with no wins is the seed's margin idea working. Marked down because DNF
is zero points and repairs cost 15 credits a hull point, which is a spiral
aimed at exactly the player who is already losing.

**Legibility 4.** The offset bar with three dots on it is the single most
readable race object in this round. One glance tells me who is fast, who is
charging and who is farming. And reaction delay landing in **my thumb** — tap
Brace on a fast ship and the shields come up two thirds of a second later — is
the only version of "speed costs handling" that a player feels rather than
infers. Against that: five phases with laps going 3/2/2/1/1 and segments going
12 to 28 means the shape of a heat changes under me four times. Two currencies,
wanted, G-load, three defence layers, declarations, placements and mods is
already full, and then the Steward's bonus adds a fifth scoring category drawn
fresh each phase that is "often the cut line". That is a thing that decides my
season and that I did not know I was playing for.

**Fit 5.** Deterministic, 30 ticks a second, integer delay, simultaneous
declarations as inputs. Three carried actives is one thumb.

**Buildable 3.** The design leans on a nav that plans: it sequences a wide
excursion to land on a bend exit, routes around a rival's debris belt, decides
when to skip a collector because the lap is going badly. That is a route
planner, and the proposal is honest that a dumb nav makes the track read as
noise. Five phases and twelve bots that build, declare, place and fly is a lot
of field.

**Measurable 5.** Names the exact first dial (240 per pool per heat) and a
concrete fail condition (mod purchase under 30% means mods are strictly bad).
That is a harness brief, not an aspiration.

**Best idea.** Route declarations revealed before placement. It converts the
shared pool from a thing you find out about afterwards into a thing you play
against, it costs one tap, and it is the cheapest interaction mechanic in the
round by a distance.

**Fatal flaw.** Nav does too much. It sets the plan, smooths acceleration,
dodges hazards and drives home crewless. When I lose, the honest explanation is
"the nav decided something", and there is no screen that can show me that
decision in the time I have. A design where an opaque planner mediates every
rating I bought is not a phone design.

**What it makes better in the seed.** The golden path. The seed says ships move
faster on it and collect energy from it and that swinging wide is dangerous and
slow. This turns that into offset, a single continuous number with a stated
speed curve and a stated charge cutoff, and then puts every credit in the game
in the band at offset 40–80. "The line is speed, the wide is money, and you
cannot be in both places" is the seed's sixth open question answered in one
sentence.

**What it drops, and was that right.** Drops three laps as a constant, shrinking
to one lap by phase 5. Right, and the only proposal to notice that three laps of
a grown track is a three-minute race. Drops gravity assists and diffuse humanoid
luck, both correctly — a 35% named roll is measurable and a luck stat is not.
But it keeps the Steward's bonus, which it invented, and that is exactly the
kind of addition this round should have cut.

## Par and Purse (season-first)

**Build 4.** Four numbers on the card, a Line dial, and the purse card giving
each week's build a question to answer. Nav tier 3 buying certainty rather than
speed is a good, unusual purchase. Marked down because the card changes every
heat while the hardware changes almost never, so the question I am asked and the
ship I can answer with are on different clocks, and the proposal does not
reconcile that.

**Tradeoffs 5.** Strain against taps, thrust against grip, path energy split
three ways against dark matter taken whole, 700 credits of certainty against 700
credits of parts, and a writ that only pays if you expect to keep losing. The
purchase _windows_ — nav is only worth it before phase 2, writs only after a cut
tells you that you will host — are the sharpest spend-or-save framing in the
round.

**Interaction 5.** The host rule is the best structural idea here: the trailing
pilot brings the track, the leader always races away from home, so track
modification becomes the recovery system instead of a private buff. Inverted
matchmaking putting rank 12 against rank 1 every round guarantees a fast
reference to stay close to. Livery tags showing three question marks for a rival
you have never met is a nice touch — an unknown field is a field you cannot
price.

**Stakes 5.** The best in the round and not close. Drop your worst heat per
phase, so one disaster is free. Margins compound with worked numbers: 18 points
from three losses beats 13 from one win and two collapses. The stagger keeps the
final from being a coronation. Every part of the seed's twelfth and eleventh
questions is answered with arithmetic.

**Legibility 2.** This is where it falls over for me. My score depends on `S`
and `W`, which change by card; on my gap in _lengths_; on whether the reference
was the winner or the par ghost; and on whether this was my dropped heat. That
is four lookups to explain one number. Worse, a **length is one twenty-fifth of
a lap and the lap grows from 3,000 to 5,000 units**, so a length is 120 units in
phase 1 and 200 in phase 3. The proposal says this makes the unit survive the
growth. It does the opposite: "four lengths back" silently means a different
amount of time in every phase, and the scoring spine is built on it. Add hold
numbers, `v²/400` against `Grip × H / 10`, strain thresholds at 30/60/90,
Instinct decaying across a range, wanted 0–10, three card types, spurs, writs,
markers, and four bars the proposal itself calls the ceiling. This is the
longest manual of the four.

**Fit 5.** Deterministic, seeded rolls, 45 to 75 second heats, two to four taps.
No complaint.

**Buildable 2.** The par ghost is a solved reference time, and it has to exist
for every course in every phase — including the host's course with their writs
on it, which changes the reference. That is not addressed and it is not small.
On top: twelve pilots, three card types, drop-worst-heat, inverted matchmaking,
host rotation, spur draws, writs, markers and a staggered final. No first
playable is named, and I do not think a small one exists without removing the
par ghost, which is the premise.

**Measurable 5.** Names its own harness test precisely — check whether a
no-input Drone Hull survives to phase 3 and drop S from 16 to 12 if it does.
That is how you write a balance question.

**Best idea.** The purse card, shown during the build minute. Randomness that
changes what you build rather than what happens to you is the correct shape for
a deterministic game, and it gives each heat a question a phone player can hold
in one sentence: this week, closeness pays, or this week, only winning does.

**Fatal flaw.** The par ghost. The entire scoring spine hangs on a published par
time, and the same design has the trailing pilot host on a course they have
permanently modified with a Debris Drift or a Bloom Node. Par on a course that
changes per host is either undefined or has to be recomputed per course per
phase, and either way the number I am scored against is one I cannot see coming.

**What it makes better in the seed.** The margin rule. The seed flags point
cutoffs as boring and asks for margins that compound into an extra stage or two,
and this is the only proposal that shows the compounding with real numbers and
then varies the _shape_ of the second-place curve so the same margin is worth
three different things depending on the week. That is a genuine development, not
a formula.

**What it drops, and was that right.** Drops gravity assists into the nav core
and narrows humanoid luck to one Instinct roll. Both right, and for the right
stated reason. Drops nothing else, and that is the problem: it keeps all six
seed systems and then adds cards, ghosts, hosting, dropped heats and inverted
matching on top. Keeping everything is not making choices.

## The Flight Card (ship-first)

**Build 5.** The best build screen in the round. Hardware sets ceilings slowly
and expensively; a free flight card set in ten seconds says how close to those
ceilings to run; and the garage **draws each ceiling as a notch on the slider**,
so overdriving is a thing you can see yourself choosing. Two players with the
same parts and different cards are two different ships. The Sealed Hull with no
berths at all is a real, chosen, coherent build.

**Tradeoffs 4.** Acceleration is thrust over mass, G is acceleration, and G
throttles how often the crew can spend charge — so bolting on a dark-matter
trawl either slows you or forces a bigger drive that pins the crew who were
meant to work the trawl. The collector taxes itself. That chain is excellent.
Marked down because the card itself is free and changes every heat, so the
per-heat decision costs nothing but risk, and there is no spend-against-save
tension beyond "tier 3 unlocks in phase 3".

**Interaction 4.** Collector loadouts visible on the grid, mods applying to
everyone in your heats, blind simultaneous mines. Kesta's Charge Siphon ending
Vetch's season three heats later, without her ship ever touching his, is the
best single interaction moment written this round. Marked down because there is
no step where I read a rival's _intent_, only their hardware — the mind game is
one-shot at the grid and then over.

**Stakes 3.** The margin formula is clean and the 90-versus-54 argument over
nine heats is convincing. But 12 to 6 after phase 1 cuts half the field after
three heats, which is sudden rather than earned, and phase 3 is three people
racing each other three times, which is a lot of season for three ships.
Recovery is "you have a bigger wallet than the leader". That is not recovery;
that is being poor with extra steps, and the proposal's own owner question
admits it.

**Legibility 4.** Several of the round's best legibility decisions are here.
There is exactly one race resource and three buttons. Hull damage is rerouted
into handling, so an emptying bar is felt as the ship missing corners rather
than as a number going down. Crew is two to four berth icons going dark, not a
bar. A ship never explodes, so I never lose to something invisible. And the
post-race report **names the exact corner where the card outran the hardware** —
that is the single best answer to "why did I lose" in this round, and it is one
line of text. Against it: `4 + 16 × (G ÷ G-tolerance)` ticks between crew
actions is a cadence, not a feeling. "My crew acts every 21 ticks" is not
something a phone player perceives. And `handling ≥ T × v² / 12` is arithmetic I
have to do in the garage across three dials with three separate ceilings.

**Fit 5.** Deterministic, 1,200-tick heats at 20 ticks a second, and most heats
need zero taps in race because the card carries the plan. This is the most
one-thumb of the four.

**Buildable 3.** Mass and thrust physics, a grip inequality per segment, a nav
that plans and can fly crewless, four mod types, mines, patrols, twelve players.
Smaller than season-first — one loop of eight segments, no ghost, no host
rotation — but no first playable is named and the mass economy touches every
part.

**Measurable 5.** A robot has zero variance and a humanoid has a stated
probability, which is a clean A/B for a harness. The card is three dials of
eleven values, which a bot can grid-search exhaustively — you can literally
compute the optimal card per build per track and see whether the hardware
choices still matter.

**Best idea.** The hardware/card split, with the hardware ceilings drawn as
notches on the card's sliders. It gives a phone player a plan three heats in the
making and a decision they can make in ten seconds while waiting for a bus, and
it makes overdriving legible as a line you can see yourself crossing.

**Fatal flaw.** Nothing happens between the lights and the flag. The card is set
in the garage, there is no declaration step, no read on a rival's intent, and
three buttons that the worked run shows being used badly once. For my five
minutes that is nearly a virtue — but the proposal asks the owner whether not
steering feels like not playing, and on the evidence of its own worked run,
where the only in-race decision is Vetch wasting a Strike, the answer is that it
does.

**What it makes better in the seed.** Acceleration generates gravity, which
impairs the crew. The seed says this and leaves "impairs" undefined. This makes
it exactly one thing: G sets how often the crew may spend charge. It is a single
number, it comes out of the build rather than being bolted on, and it makes the
seed's second question — is acceleration a cost as well as a good — answerable
with yes, it costs you your hands.

**What it drops, and was that right.** Drops hull as traditional health: no
explosion, no DNF from damage. Right, and the best cut anyone made — losing a
race to a bar emptying while I watch is the worst thing that can happen on a
phone. Drops the separate crew health bar for berths as icons. Right. Drops "no
certainty" about growth in favour of one phase of forecast on character. Right:
a hint I cannot act on is not a decision, it is decoration.

## Recommended merge

**Build from economy-first, The Ledger and the Line.** Not because it is the
most elegant — ship-first is — but because one bar does four jobs and that is
how you fit this seed on a phone. Charge is income, speed, shields and ammunition
at once, so the whole mid-race decision is a single readable question: does this
heat buy a place or a payday. Its pool rule is one sentence applied four times.
Its post-heat split screen teaches the economy without a tutorial. And it is the
only proposal that names a first playable I believe.

Graft four things onto it.

**The offset bar, from track-first.** Replace the line score and the per-corner
difficulty check with one number, 0 to 100, three dots on it, speed and charge
and credits all reading off the same axis. This is the round's best race HUD and
it costs economy-first nothing — throttle becomes the thing that sets how far
offset drifts, rather than a separate arithmetic layer.

**The declaration step, from track-first.** Declare blind, reveal, then place.
It is a better answer to the seed's ninth question than economy-first's manifest
because it tells me the contest one move before I commit rather than one heat
after, and it removes the risk economy-first names first: heats resolving on the
grid because rigs are readable. Keep the flare; it becomes the informed move.

**The named-corner post-race line, from ship-first.** One sentence: "you asked
throttle 4 for a D7 corner on lap 2 and lost 24 ticks and a charge node." That,
plus the pool split screen, is a complete answer to why I lost, in two lines,
before I put the phone away.

**Drop-worst-heat, from season-first.** One free disaster per stage. It is the
cheapest recovery rule in the round, it needs no ghost and no host rotation, and
it is the thing that lets someone play a heat badly in a five-minute gap and not
lose a season for it.

Take one thing from season-first with care: the **purse card shown during the
build**. It gives every heat a one-sentence question. But strip it to two cards,
not three, and do not let it change the scoring formula — let it change only the
_purse split_, so the points arithmetic stays identical every week.

**What I would cut, in order.**

1. **The two crew numbers.** Stamina and crew HP in economy-first are one idea
   wearing two bars. Crew is a tap budget. Take ship-first's berths — icons that
   go dark — and let damage and gravity both eat that one number. This is also
   the answer to the seed's third question: hull and shields earn their places
   because they protect different things, and crew earns its place _only_ when
   it is your hands rather than a third pool of health.
2. **Bounty, wanted and the cutters, out of the core.** Three systems to teach
   the least reliable route, and economy-first already says bounty is what it
   would cut first. Ship it as stage 2 content once the three-pool economy reads.
3. **The seat rotation.** One course per heat, the host's. Three tracks in
   45 seconds is unreadable no matter how neat the toll accounting is.
4. **The post-hoc margin divisor.** Fix `g` at 30 and publish it. A random
   target announced after the race is not a target I can race to. Use
   track-first's shape — winner 10, everyone else `max(1, 8 − floor(gap/25))` —
   one line, no card, no draw.
5. **The Steward's bonus** (track-first) and the **par ghost** (season-first).
   Both decide seasons with a number the player cannot see during the race.
6. **Gravity as anything but a tax on taps.** Never damage, never a global
   impairment. Ship-first's version — acceleration sets how often you may spend —
   is correct; keep only that, and only if it can be drawn as the charge button
   greying out.

That leaves three bars (hull, shields, charge), one dial, one offset number,
three pools, one declaration, one placement, and a points line I can say out
loud. That is a game I can hold in my head on a train, and it is still every
tension the seed asked for.
