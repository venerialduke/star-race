# Critique

1. **Nothing in the rules can kill a berth, so the whole crew layer has no cause.**
   Berths die to "radiation fields and crew-targeted fire." No segment type emits
   radiation — the field segment does 4 hull a tick — and no mount targets crew;
   the Rail Lance does hull and offset. So the crewless nav ship, the robot's
   double berth health, losing a tap per dark berth, and the Sealed Hull's
   immunity are all consequences of a damage type that does not exist. Question 4
   is answered in prose and unanswerable in the rules.

2. **The overdrive roll has no number and no failure effect, which is where the
   crew fork was supposed to live.**
   The synthesis says a humanoid "passes a seeded roll and hold anyway" and a
   robot "fails identically every time," but never states the probability or what
   failing does to Entry or Reach. Ship-first supplied `0.5 − 0.1 × overdrive`;
   the synthesis kept the mechanic and dropped the number. Worse, the only stated
   overdrive penalty is Push's (+8 ticks a tap), and the robot's Push notch is 10
   — the top of the dial — so a robot can never overdrive Push at all. The
   humanoid's one advantage exists only in a region the robot has no reason to
   enter, and the "two games" claim of question 5 rests on an unwritten rule.

3. **The one-line pool rule pays full price for one well, so the wide route
   rewards minimum effort.**
   Your cut is the pool times your share of what the field harvested. If nobody
   else harvests dark, one well is a 100% share and takes all 150 credits. In the
   worked run Iggy scoops seven wells, spends roughly 280 ticks doing it, finishes
   190 back and earns exactly what one well would have earned. The rule the
   synthesis calls its centre and praises for having no cap makes the dominant
   uncontested strategy "take one well, go back to the line" — and the declaration
   step, by telling you nobody else went Wide, is what makes that read reliable.

4. **Entry 10 dominates on time, reproducing the exact flaw the synthesis says it
   fixed.**
   Entry 10 saves 30 ticks a corner, 120 a lap on four corners — that part checks
   out. The claimed give-back does not. At Entry 10 the demand is `D × 1.0`, so
   the worst realistic shortfall against Grip 3 + Nav 3 on a D8 corner is n = 2,
   costing 12 ticks, 4 hull, and about 3 ticks of speed loss at offset 40. Two
   misses cost roughly 25 ticks against 120 saved. "Time is close to a wash" is
   off by a factor of five; Entry is not a dial, it is a setting. The rejected
   throttle dial failed for the same reason and the rejection notes say so.

5. **Three of the four taps have no stated effect, and "pre-set taps" quietly
   deletes the gravity tax.**
   Boost 25, Vent 30, Brace 15 are listed as charge prices with no consequences
   anywhere in the document; only the Lance resolves. Taps are the entire in-race
   player layer under constraint 3, so this is the hole in the middle of the one
   thumb. And the worked run has Vex with "three taps pre-set" — a mechanic
   mentioned once. If taps can be scheduled in advance, the `round(p²/(Grip+Nav))`
   delay is trivially cancelled by scheduling 13 ticks earlier, and with it the
   coupling "the fast build is worse in the player's own hands."

6. **Light is defined three different ways and the worked run uses the third.**
   The offset rule pays 1 charge per 5 ticks on the line. The segment definitions
   pay light at nodes, the Solar Sail pays "+25% light on the line," and the
   Beacon Ring "doubles a segment's light" — all per-node language. Then the
   worked run has Sabine netting 31 light on one Siphoned straight where she
   wanted 60, but a 50-tick straight is 10 charge at the stated rate, and half of
   it is 5. The heat totals (255 light in Stage 1, Vex's 148 net) are roughly
   consistent with the per-tick rate and inconsistent with the node figures. One
   of these has to go before anything is measurable.

7. **Clean pays 60 flat against a three-way solar split of 40, and no payoff
   matrix is written.**
   The declaration is the round's most praised mechanic and its economics are
   never laid out. Solar is 120: two on the line take 60 each, three take 40 each.
   Clean's flat 60 therefore matches a two-way split and beats a three-way one,
   while also removing your harvest from the pool and so raising everyone else's
   cut. Clean is a floor that dominates crowded Line and is a gift to rivals — the
   claim that it is "the fastest ship on the course" is unsupported, since Line
   caps Reach at 2 and Reach 0 buys almost nothing over that.

8. **Mods saturate the ring and the recovery lever bills the poorest player.**
   Every mod every pilot owns is on the course in every heat, one install per
   stage break and two for the bottom third. That is roughly 36 modifications
   across three breaks landing on a ring that grows from 12 to 20 segments — more
   mods than segments, with no rule for stacking, for exclusion, or for whether an
   eliminated pilot's mods stay. The bottom third's second slot costs 140–200
   credits out of a season of about 1,300, so the stated recovery mechanism is a
   compulsory purchase for the player with the least money.

9. **"Nine heats each, about twenty minutes" is true only for the winner, and the
   document never says what happens when you are cut.**
   Stage 1 is three rounds, so a pilot eliminated there races three heats and is
   done in about six minutes. The loop paragraph says "three heats, three times"
   while the season says 3 + 3 + 2 plus a Last Heat for three pilots. And "eight
   points a heat across three heats is 24" ignores the drop-worst rule, which
   leaves two counting heats and 16. Question 12's session length is answered for
   the best case only.

10. **The par ghost has no formula and never appears in the worked run.**
    Par is the reference for every margin score when the winner is slow, and it is
    "computed once at stage open" — from what card, what Nav, what Grip? Stage 1's
    par lap of 620 over 12 segments implies a specific ghost card that is never
    named, and the same 50-tick segment is described both as a fixed length and as
    a length the Push and Entry formulas rewrite. In the whole worked heat the
    ghost is never checked, so the one clause the systems reviewer called the fix
    for margin scoring is present as an assertion.

11. **Shields have no source, Thrust does not exist, and there is no starting
    bankroll.**
    Shields are given a 0–40 range and a regeneration rate, but no hull, mount or
    purchase grants any. The Gyro Rig costs "−1 Thrust" against a hull table with
    no thrust column and a straight-line formula that uses only Push, so a Barge
    and a Skiff cross a straight in identical time. Nav "1 to 5 at 60 credits a
    level" makes the flagship 550-credit ship cost 540 or 600 depending on where
    you start, and the document never says what a pilot owns or holds on day one.

12. **No first playable, and the matching rule does not produce the property
    claimed for it.**
    The synthesis credits economy-first for "the discipline of naming a first
    playable" and then does not name one, while carrying four hulls, six mounts,
    four mods, three segment types, three dials, four taps, three pools, three
    purse cards, three declarations, four seams, overdrive, berths, the ghost and
    inverted thirds. And inverted thirds — one pilot from each third — does not
    give "rank 12 races rank 1 every round" without a stated pairing, and any
    pairing that does means rank 4 never meets rank 1.

## What the next round should be asked

The synthesis names the right two jobs and both are downstream of a prior one.
Before the garage is developed or the declaration is stress-tested, the next
round should be asked to close the race model, because eight of the twelve gaps
above are one missing rule each rather than a design disagreement.

Ask for a race that resolves. Name the four taps and what each does in ticks and
damage. State the overdrive probability and, separately, what an Entry failure
and a Reach failure do. State what damages a berth, as a track element or a
mount, or delete crew as a layer and answer question 3 with two. Pick one
definition of light — per tick or per node — and rewrite the Sail, the Beacon and
the Siphon in it. Give the par ghost a card so it can be computed.

Then ask for the two dials that are currently settings rather than choices. Entry
needs a cost that scales with the corner or it is always 10; the honest fix is
probably to price the miss in ticks, not income. And the pool rule needs a floor
on effort — a minimum harvest to claim a share, or a yield cap per well — or
"take one well and leave" beats flying the wide route at all.

Finally, ask the round to write the declaration payoff matrix out: nine
combinations of three declarations, the credits each pilot takes in each, and
whether any cell is a fixed point. If Clean at 60 is a floor above a contested
Line, the declaration is solved before it is played, and that is worth knowing
before it becomes the spine of the game.
