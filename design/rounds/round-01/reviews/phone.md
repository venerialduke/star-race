# Review: Phone

I play with one thumb, in five-minute gaps, on a train. I weight legibility and
tradeoffs most, and I count systems. Every system a proposal adds is one more
thing I have to remember after a four-hour break between heat 4 and heat 5.
Every timer is a screen I lose because the train went into a tunnel.

One note on pace that applies to all four. Every rival in every proposal is a
bot. A timer on a garage full of bots is a timer against nobody. The Cut is the
only proposal with no timers, and it is the only one that fits a gap.

## The Purse

1. Build fantasy: 4. Makers at four change a rule and Slingshot four turning the black hole into a 1.15 assist is a real moment; but makers, Mk II and slots are three build systems doing one job.
2. Tradeoffs: 4. Repair, slot, part and flag all draw from one purse, so every garage is one budget question, and interest at 1 per 10 gives saving a reason.
3. Interaction: 3. The pool and the flag are real, but flags are secret until the countdown, so the course changes for reasons I only learn on the grid, and reading eight banks and maker counts on a standings table is a spreadsheet, not a glance.
4. Stakes: 4. Cuts at heats 4 and 8 are earned, a lost ship comes back at 25 hull poor rather than dead, and the loss streak is a recovery valve.
5. Legibility: 2. Five income sources: appearance, purse, hot laps, interest, streaks. After a heat I earned 7 and I cannot say why. Add secret flags, sector odds, Mk II and three buyable slots, and this is the proposal I would put down at heat 6 and not pick up.
6. Fit: 4. Rule modifiers read at race start are the honest way to do maker bonuses; the sim stays pure.
7. Buildable: 2. Sixteen to twenty PRs and five new files, and the proposal's own fallback is to ship without Mk II.
8. Measurable: 4. Saver, spender, never-repair, always-repair and flag-counter over 500 runs is the right harness, and the 35 percent test on a heat-one winner is a good number to hold.

Best idea: one coin. Repairs, slots, parts and flags all cost credits, so hull damage is a bill and greed has a price you can read.

Fatal flaw: system count. Twelve heats, twenty minutes, five income streams, three build systems and a secret flag tally. The proposal admits the build stack is one more than a phone holds and admits the pool is invisible without counts. It is right both times. The pace is wrong too: twenty minutes is four of my gaps.

I disagree with the claim that hot laps and streaks are needed as recovery. A placing purse with a floor for finishing does the same job in one line.

## The Course Is the Other Players

1. Build fantasy: 3. Levels are stacking with a name, which the sim already does. The three archetypes fall out of the seed table rather than being designed, and the only comes-online moment is a Mk III seed spanning two slots.
2. Tradeoffs: 3. Income is flat on purpose, and the proposal says the gap between players is run hull, not money. That is half the brief's second gap left open. Greed early is nearly always right by its own account.
3. Interaction: 5. This is the reason to read it. A part is a shield and a weapon at once, the course is built by the players every heat, and every hazard carries the name of who laid it.
4. Stakes: 4. The run hull table is plain arithmetic, nobody is out before heat 3, and fifth every heat scrapes home. Standing as health with ship hull repaired between heats is the right split.
5. Legibility: 3. Attribution is the most legible single thing in all four proposals: "18 hull to Bulwark's rock" is exactly what I need to see in ten seconds. Everything around it is not. Six seeds times three levels is eighteen hazard variants I have to know to read a course. Two seeding waves on ten-second timers with a tie rule is a desktop moment. The star's dissipation curve is one number per heat that changes what every part is worth, invisibly.
6. Fit: 4. Seeds, buys and rerolls are all inputs, the sim stays pure, and the timed phases live in the UI. But the timers are the wrong call for a phone whatever layer they live in.
7. Buildable: 2. Eighteen PRs, twelve to playable, and the proposal calls itself S2 and S3 again. That is honest and it is a lot.
8. Measurable: 4. A fixed seeder against a ribbon reader is the right test for whether seeding degenerates.

Best idea: attribution. Every hazard names who laid it, and the results screen reads hazard by hazard. This is the one thing every merge should take.

Fatal flaw: it cannot be held in one head on a phone. The proposal says "two hulls are two systems to hold in one head" and then adds a star curve, an eighteen-entry seed table and a two-wave seeding phase on timers. Right diagnosis, wrong prescription.

## The Cut

1. Build fantasy: 4. Two of a tag is a direction and three changes a rule, twelve parts in three tiers, and a greedy ship can hold two sets by the final. It lacks the second tier that Hardpoints' overdrive gives, so the build has a middle but no late.
2. Tradeoffs: 4. Tier gating is the best save reason in the round: the part that completes your set costs 4 or 6 the heat it appears, and a ship that spent 2 a heat on tier 1 cannot afford its own plan. Repairs at 1 per 20 hull compete with the draft for the same credits. The line "rich in seventh place and about to be cut" is the whole tension in nine words.
3. Interaction: 4. Leader lays the course, tail shops first, the draft is shared and visible, denial is a legal move. Seven of eight ships do nothing to the track each heat, which is less than interaction-first offers and exactly as much as I can follow.
4. Stakes: 4. One cut per heat from heat 3, the line drawn on the ladder, a crash is a zero and a return at 40 hull. The ladder is relative, so a bad heat can be climbed back from, which Hardpoints' standing cannot.
5. Legibility: 5. Three screens in a fixed order, each one or two taps, no timers: ladder, course, draft. Everything is public. Points are 8 down to 1, the purse is 6, 5, 4, 3, interest is 1 per 4. I can say after any heat exactly why I have what I have. The season is drawn end to end in the lobby, so the black hole in heat 4 is a plan, not a surprise.
6. Fit: 5. A rules block resolved beside the stats, race.ts reading five numbers from the build instead of tuning, and hazards, actives and pilot untouched. Nothing bends.
7. Buildable: 4. Thirteen PRs, nine to playable, with the biggest change confined to run.ts. Believable.
8. Measurable: 4. Save, spend, set-chase, counter-pick and deny bots, and the stated test that a runaway leader after heat 3 is caught a third of the time.

Best idea: the three fixed screens. Ladder, course, draft, in that order, one glance each. That is the shape of a between-round on a phone, and no other proposal has it.

Fatal flaw: none. Two things to fix. Leader-lays-course is a snowball, and the worked run shows it: Redline lays heats 2 to 5 for itself, then the player lays two black holes that end Halo. The proposal's own counterweights are tail-shops-first and the double final, and the harness has to prove them. Second, the pace claim is wrong in the good direction: seven heats of 15 seconds and six garages of three taps is 8 to 9 minutes, not 14, and that is the right length. Also, do not name a tag and a rival both Sling.

I disagree that Ledger taking Grav Keel because it was expensive is "the moment". It is a bot with a bad rule being annoying. The moment is the tail-shops-first draft that let the player take it back a heat later.

## Hardpoints

1. Build fantasy: 5. Cores at three and overdrives at five are the best comes-online in the round, the six-slot arithmetic makes archetypes something you can count on your fingers, and Gravity overdrive diving the black hole everyone else crawls through is the fantasy the brief asked for.
2. Tradeoffs: 4. Hardpoints at 6, 8 and 10 credits are a clean long bet that buys nothing this heat; interest at 1 per 10; and the stated test that the greedy bot wins sector 1 and the banker sector 3 is the right one. But the worked run's banker is eliminated at round 6 having banked to 40, which says the tradeoff is not balanced yet.
3. Interaction: 4. Everyone drops a beacon every round, the family decides which, denial is priced at 2 credits and a round, and the pool bars say who is crowded. The pip strip is the best build-reading device in the round.
4. Stakes: 3. Standing starts at 20 and only goes down. The recovery levers are credits and drop order, not standing, so a bad sector 1 is permanent. The arithmetic that fifth every round ends at exactly 0 is tidy for a tuner and invisible to a player.
5. Legibility: 3. Four colours of pip, core at 3, overdrive at 5: that part is holdable. Eight beacons per round on three segments, three per segment, on a course that already escalates by sector, is not. Plus bridges that count twice, sector adders on standing loss, and two timed screens.
6. Fit: 4. Ten modifiers as a hard ceiling is a good discipline. Beacons laid into a track so the sim never knows who dropped what is the right shape.
7. Buildable: 3. Fourteen PRs, seven to playable, but ten modifiers threaded through hazards and race is more than the proposal's "reads three of them" suggests.
8. Measurable: 4. Six archetypes plus greedy, banker and switcher, and the check that no family is unreachable in more than a few percent of runs.

Best idea: coloured pips with a count. Three red pips is a Burn ship one part from its core. That is a build read in under a second, and it should be on every ladder screen in the game.

Fatal flaw: the beacon pile. Eight players each drop one beacon per round, up to three on a segment, each doubled by a core. By round 5 the course is a stack of labelled boxes I cannot read in the 15-second drop window, and the proposal's own risk section says the cap may have to go to one. It should start at one. The rock-paper-scissors "nobody has to be taught" is a four by four matrix, and it does have to be taught.

## Recommended merge

Build from The Cut. It is the only proposal I can play in a gap: no timers, three screens between heats, everything public, a season drawn in the lobby, and a run that is 8 to 9 minutes by its own numbers. Its ladder is a relative health that can be climbed, which the brief asked for and Hardpoints' standing cannot do.

Graft four things onto it.

From interaction-first, attribution. The leader's card carries the leader's name, and the results screen reads "lost 30 hull to Redline's asteroid field, cooked 2 seconds at the ringed planet you dropped". That is the whole of "understand why you lost in the time it takes to show it", and The Cut does not have it.

From Hardpoints, coloured pips. The Cut already has tags and set counts on the ladder as "Burner 2/3". Make the tags four colours and the counts pips. Nothing else changes.

From Hardpoints, the overdrive tier, later. The Cut's set comes online at 3 and stops. A second threshold at 5 gives the build a late game. Do not do it in the first playable; do it when the harness says sets at 3 are balanced.

From interaction-first, tie the leader's card to the leader's build, if the snowball proves real. Instead of three cards from a deck, the leader chooses from the hazards its own sets answer. The course then reads from the ribbon, and a Brick leader dropping rock is a thing the whole field saw coming.

Take nothing from The Purse but the discipline: one coin for parts and repairs, which The Cut already has. Leave hot laps, streaks, slots, Mk II and secret flags where they are.

Two calls the owner has to make before a PR is opened. Keep the run at seven heats; do not stretch it to nine or twelve. Cap between-heat screens at three and refuse a fourth.
