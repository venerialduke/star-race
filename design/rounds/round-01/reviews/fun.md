# Review: Fun

The question I asked of each proposal is the one I ask of any auto-battler after a hundred hours: is there a plan, does the plan take rounds to come together, does it hurt to be wrong about it, and do the other seven people at the table matter to it. Everything below is weighted toward those four. Legibility, fit, cost and measurability are scored honestly but they did not decide my recommendation.

| Proposal                                            | Build fantasy | Tradeoffs | Interaction | Stakes and recovery | Legibility | Fit | Buildable | Measurable | Total |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | --------- | ----------- | ------------------- | ---------- | --- | --------- | ---------- | ----- |
| The Purse (economy-first)                           | 3             | 4         | 2           | 3                   | 2          | 4   | 2         | 4          | 24    |
| The Course Is the Other Players (interaction-first) | 4             | 2         | 5           | 4                   | 2          | 4   | 2         | 4          | 27    |

On the four criteria I weight most, the order is Hardpoints 17, The Cut 15, The Course 15, The Purse 12. On the total, The Cut edges Hardpoints because it is cheaper and cleaner. My recommendation follows the weighted order, not the total, and the merge section says why.

## The Purse

**Build fantasy 3.** Maker four is a real rule change and Slingshot four turning the black hole into an assist is a genuine moment, but it lands at heat six or seven of twelve, and maker two is a stat, not a plan. Mk II on top of makers on top of slots is three build systems fighting for the same attention.

**Tradeoffs 4.** The best economy on paper: repairs against parts, slots against parts, reroll against bank, and a finite pool that punishes the saver. Five income sources is two too many, and interest capped at 50 banked is a number nobody on 6 a heat will reach without sitting out the run.

**Interaction 2.** The flag is a vote. One flag in a tally of nine or ten rarely decides anything, and the proposal admits the equilibrium in its own words: everyone flags the burst. The pool with visible counts and public banks are good; the pacing lap answer is not.

**Stakes and recovery 3.** Two cuts in twelve heats, each of two ships, the first at heat four. Between cuts nothing is at risk but points. The loss streak paying from the third bad heat is charity from the game, not a lever the player pulls.

**Legibility 2.** Appearance, purse, hot laps, interest, streaks; shop, slots, repairs, flags; makers, Mk II, sector odds, a secret tally shown at the grid. This is TFT with every knob left on, and it will not fit on a phone first time.

**Fit 4.** Per-ship rule modifiers read at race start is the right way to bend hazards without touching them. Twenty minutes strains "short".

**Buildable 2.** Sixteen to twenty PRs, and the first playable version has neither flags nor makers, so it has no interaction and no build direction.

**Measurable 4.** The harness is named with concrete bots, and "a heat-one winner wins the run under 35%" is exactly the kind of target the brief asks for.

**Best idea.** The shop shows copies remaining ("Ion Thruster, 2 left"). That one line makes a private shop a contested one, and every other proposal should steal it.

**Fatal flaw.** The worked run ends with "she wins because three ships agreed on one flag in one garage". That is not a plan coming together. That is a vote going her way. The interaction system this proposal was built around produces coincidence, not counter-play.

## The Course Is the Other Players

**Build fantasy 4.** Three archetypes fall out of the seed table without being taught, and a Mk III at heat four is a clear coming-online moment. It loses a point because the build is six parts on four hardpoints and the only question is which one to triple; identity is thinner than a family system.

**Tradeoffs 2.** Everything costs 3, income is 5 to 8, ship hull is repaired free, and there is no slot or level sink. The proposal says "the gap is run hull, not money", which is another way of saying money is not a decision. The Special Order is the one real reason to hold 6.

**Interaction 5.** This is the best answer any proposal gives to the brief's question. The course is entirely the players', hazards have memory so a field in front of a hole is a combo, the bottom wave places with information, and every hit has a name on it. The heat two moment, your rock in front of Gravitas's hole taking Redline, is the moment I would show someone to sell the game.

**Stakes and recovery 4.** Run hull by position with the star's multiplier is earned, nobody is out before heat three, and a crash costs one heat. It loses a point because a slow leak has no line drawn under it; a player at 27 run hull is dying, not fighting.

**Legibility 2.** Eight player-laid hazards on a 28-second course, some twinned, some spanning two slots, from a seed table with eighteen variants, placed in two ten-second waves. Attribution and the ribbon are the right tools, but they are patching a course that is too busy to read.

**Fit 4.** Seeds are inputs, dissipation is an option, and the pilot seeds by the same rule a human does. Nothing is bent. The seeding timer is UI and belongs there.

**Buildable 2.** Eighteen PRs, twelve before anything is playable. The proposal calls it S2 and S3 again and it is right.

**Measurable 4.** The bot seeding rule lives beside the tap rules, and the degenerate-course test (always-seed-the-same versus reads-the-ribbon) is the right test to write first.

**Best idea.** Sequence. A hazard is stronger because of what came before it, and the player who places last can build the combo. That is the trailer's weapon and the comeback in one mechanic, and it costs nothing in the sim.

**Fatal flaw.** Everyone lays what they survive, and rock is a punishment while a straight is only a reward. The proposal's own logic points at a lobby that converges on Plating by heat five: rock everywhere, speed a trap, and the game becomes who has the most armour. The harness may prove me wrong. Until it does, this is a course that eats its own variety.

## The Cut

**Build fantasy 4.** Sets change a rule at three, tiers gate what can complete a set, and the season map in the lobby lets you pick Sling because you can see the black hole in heat four. It loses a point because seven heats means online at four and three heats of payoff, and a second set is a dream almost nobody reaches.

**Tradeoffs 3.** Tier gating is a good reason to bank and repair-or-part is a real fork. But the purse is a flat 3 for fourth through eighth, interest at 1 per 4 rewards banking almost immediately, and there are no rerolls, slots or levels, so the economy has one axis.

**Interaction 3.** The face-up draft in reverse ladder order is the most legible denial in any of the four; "Halo picks before me, that part is gone" is exactly the read a phone player can make. But the course lever belongs to one player per heat. Seven of eight ships do nothing to the track. Most rounds, the only way another player touches my race is by taking a card.

**Stakes and recovery 5.** A cut line drawn on the ladder, one ship gone after every heat from the third, tiebreak on the most recent heat so the ship falling is the ship that goes. This is the tension the brief asked for and nobody else delivers it. Tail shops first is structural recovery, not a handout.

**Legibility 4.** Ladder, course card, draft, in a fixed order, each a tap or two. Twelve parts, four tags. A phone can hold this.

**Fit 5.** A rules block on the resolved build and five reads in race.ts. The hazards and taps are untouched. Nothing is bent and nothing needs to be.

**Buildable 4.** Thirteen PRs, nine to playable, and most of the sim survives. The honest count in the proposal matches what the code looks like.

**Measurable 4.** Save, spend, set-chase, counter-pick and deny bots, and "a runaway leader after heat 3 is caught a third of the time" is a target the harness can check.

**Best idea.** The ladder with the cut line under it. Every point I take is a point the ship near the line did not, and sixth instead of seventh in heat five is a real, visible, one-row decision.

**Fatal flaw.** No single one. The nearest is that leader-lays-course means the field is scenery for seven players out of eight on the track, and the proposal knows it: the owner question "leader or tail" is really "why not everyone".

## Hardpoints

**Build fantasy 5.** Cores at three, overdrives at five, six hardpoints that make the archetypes arithmetic, bridges that count twice, coloured pips that show everyone what you are. This is TFT traits done for a racer, and the moment Kestrel goes to Gravity overdrive and dives the black hole everyone else is crawling through is the best coming-online moment in the round.

**Tradeoffs 4.** Hardpoints at 6, 8 and 10 are the level-up sink, and an overdrive costs about 29 credits by round six on 5 to 8 a round, so greed in sector one against banking for sector three is a real choice. It loses a point because sector gates repair free, so hull never costs money, and selling for 1 having paid 3 makes a pivot a punishment rather than a choice.

**Interaction 4.** Everyone drops a beacon every round, the family decides the beacon, the pool has six copies and the bars show it, and the tail drops last with full information. Halo's Ion Storm on a corridor with two bursts, made stick by the Fins you bought to deny Redline, is a proper two-player play. A point off because a 60-tick beacon is a nudge, not a wall, and three per segment is either unreadable or has to go to one.

**Stakes and recovery 4.** Standing 20 with sector multipliers, and "fifth every round ends on exactly 0" is a good tuning anchor. It loses a point for the same reason The Course does: a drain has no line under it, so the mid-table leaks rather than fights, and elimination only bites in sector three.

**Legibility 3.** Pips make builds readable at a glance, which is most of the battle. Against that: four families with two thresholds each, two bridges, hardpoints, four beacon hazards, and a standing loss table with sector modifiers. Holdable, not simple.

**Fit 4.** Ten modifiers out of resolveBuild is honest, and the track function that lays beacons keeps the sim ignorant of who dropped what. The Burn overdrive "never cooks, runs faster hot" is a rule bend the proposal flags and I would cut.

**Buildable 3.** Fourteen PRs, seven to playable. The first playable has families, credits and standing but no beacons, which is a fine game already.

**Measurable 4.** Six archetype bots plus greedy, banker and switcher, and the expected shape (greedy wins sector one, banker sector three, switcher both by a little) is stated so the harness can disagree.

**Best idea.** "Six slots make the archetypes arithmetic." Five of one plus a loose part, or three and three, and no room for three cores. The commitment is built into the hull, not into a rule, and by round five the grid tells everyone what you are.

**Fatal flaw.** None fatal. The real risk is the one the proposal names: the pool is 90 copies for 60 purchases and three flex pilots on the same family starve the human. That is a wishlist rule and a harness check, not a redesign.

## Recommended merge

Build from Hardpoints. It is the only proposal where the build is the reason to play: a family total you are counting toward, a hull that forces commitment, and an overdrive that changes what a hazard is. The Cut scores one point higher in total by being cheaper and cleaner, but the thing it is cheap and clean about is a thinner version of the same set system. Take Hardpoints' families, six hardpoints, pool, pips and beacons as the spine.

Graft The Cut's ladder onto it, replacing the standing drain. Keep standing as the number, keep the sector multipliers, but draw a line: after every round from the fourth to the seventh, the lowest standing is cut, and four ships fly rounds eight and nine with the last worth double. A leak is not a stake. A line is. The tiebreak on the most recent round goes on screen beside the line, as The Cut says.

Graft The Cut's tier gating, so hardpoints are not the only reason to bank: tier two parts enter the shop from round four, tier three from round seven, at 3, 5 and 7 credits. And once per sector, at the gate, replace the private shop with The Cut's face-up draft of ten in reverse standing order. That is the carousel, it gives the tail a real pick, and it puts denial on a screen where a phone player can see it happen.

Graft two things from The Course. First, attribution: every beacon carries the name of who dropped it and the results screen reads "18 hull to Bulwark's debris". It costs a field on a placement and it is the difference between rivals and scenery. Second, sequence: keep the three-per-segment cap but lay beacons in drop order along the segment, so the last player to drop can put a flare after a shadow on purpose. If the drop screen cannot show the result plainly, cut to one per segment before cutting attribution.

From The Purse take one line: the shop shows copies remaining per part, not only a bar per family.

Cut from Hardpoints: the Burn overdrive as written. "Never cooks, runs 15% faster hot" is the balance grenade the proposal admits it is; give Burn five a second reroute per stage instead, the same shape as Field five. Do not take The Purse's flags, The Course's free hull repair, or The Cut's leader-only course card. The first is a vote, the second removes the F-Zero trade the brief asks for, and the third leaves seven players with nothing to do on the track.

The first playable version is Hardpoints' seven PRs plus the ladder cut, about eight. Beacons with attribution are the next three. The draft and tiers are the three after that. Fourteen or fifteen total, and a game with a plan, a line and a name on every hit by the end of it.
